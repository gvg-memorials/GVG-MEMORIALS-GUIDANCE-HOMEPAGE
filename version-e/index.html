Choosing a memorial

Whether you've lost a parent, a spouse, or a child, you don't have to know where to start. A name and a cemetery are all we need to begin. These five steps walk through every choice, in the order they come.

The guide

Written by our family, checked against what cemeteries actually require.

Tell us where they rest

Step one

Every cemetery has its own requirements: the type of memorial, the size, the material, and whether vases, portraits, or emblems are permitted. Share the cemetery name, and section and lot if you have them. If you don't have the paperwork, we can contact the cemetery for you.

Choose the memorial

Step two

There are four types: flat granite, which sits level with the lawn; flat bronze, a cast plate on a granite base; slant, angled to be read standing; and upright, a headstone on its own base where the cemetery permits one.

Single or companion

Step two, continued

Most memorials honor one person. A companion memorial is for two people, such as a husband and wife. One side is engraved now and the other later, at our shop or at the cemetery, depending on the cemetery's requirements. Any size can be made either way.

Choose the stone and design

Step three

Eighteen granite colors, with real samples at our shop. Our album holds 286 designs, arranged by subject, and if what you picture isn't there, we'll draw it. Ceramic portraits and bronze emblems are available where your cemetery permits them.

Receive your free written quote

Step four

Cost depends on the type, size, granite, artwork, and your cemetery's requirements. Every item is clearly listed in writing before you move forward. The quote is free, with no obligation.

Approve your proof

Step five

We draw the full layout to scale and send it to you. Review every name, date, and detail, ideally against a document. Engraving begins after you approve and sign the proof.

From proof to placement

After you approve

We send the approved layout to your cemetery for its records and required review. Most cemeteries set the memorial themselves; at Conejo Mountain Memorial Park, we install it ourselves. Once we know your cemetery and your choices, we'll give you a clear timeline.

Adding a date, or restoring a stone

An existing memorial

If your loved one is joining someone already resting, we can add the final date or a new name. We also clean and restore older stones. Send a photo of the stone and the cemetery name, and we'll explain what's possible.

What to bring

Before you visit

Whatever you have: the cemetery name and plot, their full name and dates, any words or a photo you'd like included, and any cemetery paperwork. If something is missing, we can still begin. We can meet at our shop or at your home.

GVG Memorials

From the family that makes them

GVG Memorials is a family-owned memorial company in Oxnard, California, serving Ventura County. It makes flat granite and bronze memorials, slants, custom uprights, single and companion memorials, and granite benches, and adds final dates and restores older stones.

History

The company was founded in 1998 by Gerardo Valle Garcia and Gerardo Garcia Jr. It was among the first in the area to place a color photograph on a granite memorial. Gerardo “Jerry” Garcia Jr. died in 2023; his vision continues to guide the company. Today Jerry Garcia carries the work forward as the third generation, working with carefully selected craftsmen and designers.
